Practical AI Use at SMCC

Faculty/Staff 60-Minute Intro

Internal planning draftNot official policyRequires review
Facilitator notes

Open by naming the draft status and review requirement. Set a practical tone: this is about shared language, safe boundaries, and human judgment.

Why SMCC Is Doing This

  • AI adoption is a student success, workforce readiness, and institutional capacity issue.
  • Different fields carry different risks and benefits.
  • Faculty and staff need shared language before broad use.
  • The effort starts with agency, privacy, practical workflows, and reviewable work.
Facilitator notes

Invite participants to name where AI feels useful, risky, overhyped, or unclear in their own work.

Core Messages

  • AI should enhance learning, not replace learning.
  • Prompting is task specification plus verification.
  • Human judgment remains accountable.
  • Do not enter confidential/regulated data into unapproved tools.
  • SMCC should not use AI detector scores as evidence for academic misconduct.
Facilitator notes

Return to these messages throughout the session. They are the backbone for activities and discussion.

What AI Is and Is Not

  • Generative AI produces text, images, code, summaries, plans, or other outputs based on patterns in data.
  • It can assist with drafting, organizing, and critique.
  • It is not an authority, policy owner, privacy officer, instructor of record, or accountable decision-maker.
  • Outputs can be inaccurate, biased, incomplete, or overconfident.
Facilitator notes

Use a low-risk public example if demonstrating. Ask what looks useful, then what could be wrong or unsafe.

From Chatbots to Copilots to Agents

  • A chatbot responds to prompts in a conversational interface.
  • A copilot is embedded into a work tool to assist with drafting, summarizing, coding, searching, or organizing.
  • Agentic AI may plan, retrieve information, use tools, or coordinate multiple steps under human supervision.
  • Candidate agent ideas are candidate concepts pending owner review.
Facilitator notes

Stress that the boundaries are blurring for users, which makes governance, data boundaries, and supervision more important.

Guardrail: Human Judgment Remains Accountable

  • AI can draft or organize work, but people remain responsible for final decisions.
  • Students should be able to explain choices, evidence, and revisions.
  • Staff should verify outputs before another person sees or acts on them.
  • Faculty and staff expertise is central, not optional.
Facilitator notes

Ask participants to identify one decision in their work that must remain human-owned.

Guardrail: Data Boundaries

CategoryRule of thumb
PublicPublic SMCC pages, public event information, published catalog language.
InternalNon-sensitive drafts, meeting agendas, generic workflow notes.
ConfidentialStudent records, grades, advising notes, financial aid information, employee data.
RegulatedFERPA records, disability/accommodation data, clinical-like cases, public safety incident data.
Never enterPasswords, SSNs, protected records, medical identifiers, active incident details.
Facilitator notes

Public does not automatically mean free to upload; copyrighted or licensed materials may still have restrictions.

Activity 1: Data Boundary Sort

  1. Sort examples as public, internal, confidential, regulated, or never-enter.
  2. Discuss which examples were clearly safe.
  3. Identify examples that felt tempting but risky.
  4. Name what would need to be removed, changed, or approved.

Facilitator notes

If time is tight, prioritize this activity. It creates shared vocabulary for the rest of the session.

AI-Assisted Work Quality

  • Poor work: broad prompt, large unverified output, no sources, no limits, no explanation.
  • Professional work: clear context, explicit limits, sources/tests/checks, human explanation, small reviewable output.
  • The goal is not fancy prompting; the goal is trustworthy, reviewable work.
Facilitator notes

Use a synthetic sample so participants can critique quality without privacy risk.

CLEAR Framework

ElementStandard
ContextDefine the task, audience, constraints, and success criteria
LimitsDefine what AI may and may not do
EvidenceRequire sources, tests, calculations, examples, or verification
AccountabilityThe human remains responsible for the final work
ReviewabilityThe output must be small enough, clear enough, and documented enough for another person to review
Facilitator notes

Keep the wording intact. CLEAR is the bridge between classroom assignments and staff workflows.

Activity 2: Poor vs CLEAR AI Work

  • Review the poor sample first.
  • Ask what makes it hard to trust or review.
  • Compare it with the CLEAR version.
  • Name what changed and what still needs human review.

Facilitator notes

Connect participant observations to Context, Limits, Evidence, Accountability, and Reviewability.

Academic Integrity Without AI Detector Scores

SMCC should not use AI detector scores as evidence for academic misconduct.

  • AI detection tools are probabilistic and can be wrong.
  • Detector risks are especially serious for multilingual writers and other students whose writing may not match detector assumptions.
  • Academic integrity should rely on assignment design, process evidence, student explanation, instructor judgment, and due process.
Facilitator notes

Preserve this position strongly. Do not soften it into a recommendation to use detectors cautiously as evidence.

Assignment / Workflow Triage

Faculty

  • AI prohibited
  • AI limited
  • AI allowed with documentation
  • AI integrated as workplace simulation

Staff

  • No AI
  • AI draft only
  • AI with verification
  • Candidate concept pending owner review
Facilitator notes

Practice language only. Policy categories and course-use language remain pending SMCC governance review.

Activity 3: Triage One Assignment or Workflow

  1. Choose one assignment or workflow.
  2. Select an AI use category.
  3. Run a data boundary check.
  4. Apply CLEAR.
  5. Name the human review step and next action.

Facilitator notes

Faculty can use an assignment; staff can use a workflow. Avoid real sensitive examples during practice.

Reflection / Exit Ticket

  • One AI use I may try.
  • One AI use I will avoid.
  • One privacy or policy question.
  • One assignment/workflow I want to revise.
  • Confidence level after this session: low / medium / high.
Facilitator notes

Collect concerns and next steps. This gives the AI adoption team practical signal for follow-up support.

Follow-Up Options

  • Department assignment/workflow clinic.
  • Pathway-specific AI examples lab.
  • Privacy and data guardrail studio.
  • Student-facing AI literacy planning session.
  • Employer testimonial and discipline-example review.

Close by reinforcing that these materials need owner and governance review before workshop or leadership use.
