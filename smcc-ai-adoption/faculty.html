Faculty Handout

Practical AI guidance for SMCC courses

Faculty Agency Framing

Faculty agency matters because academic judgment belongs closest to the course, the discipline, and the learner. A useful SMCC AI strategy should help faculty decide when AI is allowed, limited, prohibited, or required as a workplace simulation. Policy-related language is pending SMCC governance review.

AI should enhance learning, not replace learning.

AI Use Scale

Sample course-use categories pending SMCC governance review and faculty/department review.

ScaleMeaning
AI prohibitedStudents do the work without generative AI because the purpose is direct practice of the underlying skill.
AI limitedStudents may use AI for brainstorming, planning, or feedback, but submitted work must be their own.
AI allowed with documentationStudents may use AI for specified steps and must disclose, document, and verify the use.
AI integrated as workplace simulationAI use is required because the learning goal includes supervised AI-assisted professional work.

Course Policy Language Templates

AI prohibited for this assignment

For this assignment, do not use generative AI tools to produce, revise, translate, summarize, calculate, code, or otherwise complete any part of the submitted work unless I explicitly authorize it in writing. The purpose of this task is to practice the underlying skill directly.

AI limited for this assignment

You may use AI for brainstorming, planning, or feedback, but your submitted work must be your own. Include a short note naming the tool used, what you used it for, and one change you made after evaluating its output.

AI allowed with documentation

You may use AI as a collaborator for specified steps. Submit your prompt/output log, identify which parts of the work were AI-assisted, and explain how you checked accuracy, bias, and fit with course criteria.

AI integrated as workplace simulation

This assignment requires AI use because the learning goal includes supervised AI-assisted work. You are responsible for verifying outputs, protecting data, explaining decisions, and producing the final professional judgment.

AI Detection Position

SMCC should not use AI detector scores as evidence for academic misconduct.

AI detection tools are probabilistic and can be wrong. Detector risks are especially serious for multilingual writers and other students whose writing may not match detector assumptions. Academic integrity should rely on assignment design, process evidence, student explanation, instructor judgment, and due process.

Assignment Redesign Checklist

  • What is the actual learning goal?
  • What student thinking, practice, or performance must remain visible?
  • Is AI prohibited, limited, allowed with documentation, or integrated as workplace simulation?
  • What process evidence will students submit?
  • What sources, tests, calculations, drafts, logs, or explanations are required?
  • What privacy or copyright boundaries apply?
  • How will students show final human judgment?

CLEAR AI-Assisted Work

Prompting is task specification plus verification.

ElementStandard
ContextDefine the task, audience, constraints, and success criteria
LimitsDefine what AI may and may not do
EvidenceRequire sources, tests, calculations, examples, or verification
AccountabilityThe human remains responsible for the final work
ReviewabilityThe output must be small enough, clear enough, and documented enough for another person to review

AI-Assisted Work Rubric

CriterionLook for
Task understoodThe work addresses the task, audience, constraints, and success criteria.
AI use disclosed where requiredThe student names how AI was used and what changed after review.
Output verifiedClaims, calculations, procedures, code, or recommendations are checked.
Sources/tests/checks includedThe submission includes sources, tests, calculations, examples, or verification notes.
Human judgment visibleThe student explains choices and takes responsibility for the final work.
Work is reviewableThe work is scoped, organized, documented, and clear.
Risks/limitations namedThe student identifies uncertainty, safety issues, bias, privacy limits, or needed human review.

Discipline and Pathway Examples

  • Computer Science / IT / Cybersecurity: AI-assisted code must include small diffs, tests, explanation, security review, and official documentation checks.
  • Architectural & Engineering Design / CAD: AI-assisted design research must include verified codes, constraints, assumptions, and source-backed design decisions.
  • Health Sciences: AI-assisted patient education must be checked for accuracy, readability, scope, bias, and safety. AI must not provide diagnosis or replace clinical judgment.
  • Public Safety / EMS / Fire: AI-generated incident scenarios may support training, but procedures and command decisions must be verified against approved doctrine and instructor judgment.
  • Business / Hospitality / Culinary: AI-assisted communication, menu planning, or business analysis must be checked for accuracy, audience fit, bias, cost assumptions, and safety requirements.
  • Writing / Humanities: AI can support brainstorming and revision, but students must show source evaluation, authorship decisions, revision history, and final human judgment.

Quick Privacy Reminders

  • Do not enter confidential or regulated data into unapproved tools.
  • Do not upload identifiable student work without a clear educational purpose, disclosure, and approved data handling.
  • Do not enter grades, advising notes, financial aid information, disability/accommodation data, clinical-like real cases, or public safety incident data into external AI tools.
  • Use public, synthetic, or fully de-identified examples for practice.

Reflection Questions

  • Where might AI make learning better in my course?
  • Where might AI make learning worse?
  • What should students still be able to do without AI?
  • What evidence would show that students learned, not merely generated?
  • What AI-use language should I add to my syllabus or next assignment, pending governance review?
