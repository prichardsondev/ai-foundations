Key Guardrails

  • Do not enter confidential or regulated data into unapproved tools.
  • SMCC should not use AI detector scores as evidence for academic misconduct.
  • Candidate agents/automations are concepts pending owner review.

Leadership Brief

AI adoption at SMCC should be framed as a student success, workforce readiness, and institutional capacity initiative. The plan begins with faculty agency, staff expertise, student learning, employer expectations, privacy, and practical workflows.

Five Guiding Principles

  1. Faculty and staff agency: do not say "you must use AI."
  2. Student success: preserve learning, practice, explanation, and human judgment.
  3. Workforce readiness: use employer input to identify field-specific AI expectations.
  4. Privacy and data governance: do not enter confidential or regulated data into unapproved tools.
  5. Reviewable work: teach prompting as task specification plus verification, not magic wording.

Leadership Decisions Needed

  • Which governance bodies need to review AI guidance before workshops?
  • What tools are approved, tolerated, blocked, or under review?
  • Who owns AI privacy/data classification guidance?
  • Which faculty/staff groups should pilot the first workshop?
  • Which employer partners should be approached for testimonials?
  • Which candidate agent or workflow concepts are appropriate for owner review?

Open Placeholders Needing Confirmation

  • Named SMCC/MCCS approval owners
  • Approved/tolerated/blocked AI tools
  • Data classification process
  • Governance review path
  • Consent/release process for employer testimonials
  • Whether these materials can be shared beyond the AI adoption team
