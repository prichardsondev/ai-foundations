Workshop Activities

Data boundary cards, Poor vs CLEAR AI work samples, and assignment/workflow triage

Activity 1: Data Boundary Cards

Use these cards in the 60-minute intro session. All examples are public, synthetic, or de-identified. Do not add real student, employee, clinical, financial aid, disability, HR, or public safety incident data.

Facilitator Directions

  1. Put participants in pairs or small groups.
  2. Give each group the category list and example cards.
  3. Ask groups to sort each card into one category.
  4. Debrief: "What made this easy or hard to classify?"
  5. Reinforce: do not enter confidential/regulated data into unapproved tools.

Categories

CategoryRule of thumb
PublicPublic SMCC pages, public event information, published catalog language, public job postings.
InternalNon-sensitive drafts, meeting agendas, generic workflow notes.
ConfidentialStudent education records, grades, advising notes, financial aid information, employee data, student work with identifiers.
RegulatedFERPA-covered records, disability/accommodation data, clinical-like cases tied to real people, public safety incident data.
Never enterPasswords, SSNs, account data, medical identifiers, active incident details, confidential investigations.

Public does not automatically mean free to upload; copyrighted or licensed materials may still have restrictions.

Debrief Prompts

  • Which examples were clearly safe?
  • Which examples felt tempting but risky?
  • What would need to be removed or changed before using AI?
  • Who would need to approve the use?

Activity 2: Poor vs CLEAR AI Work Samples

Use these synthetic examples in the 60-minute intro session. They are not official SMCC policy or course requirements.

Facilitator Directions

  1. Give participants the poor sample first.
  2. Ask: "What would make this hard to trust or review?"
  3. Give participants the CLEAR version.
  4. Ask: "What changed, and what would still need human review?"
  5. Connect the discussion to Context, Limits, Evidence, Accountability, and Reviewability.

CLEAR Reminder

ElementStandard
ContextDefine the task, audience, constraints, and success criteria
LimitsDefine what AI may and may not do
EvidenceRequire sources, tests, calculations, examples, or verification
AccountabilityThe human remains responsible for the final work
ReviewabilityThe output must be small enough, clear enough, and documented enough for another person to review

Sample A: Poor AI-Assisted Work

Prompt:

Write my customer email about the program.

Submitted output:

Thanks for your interest. This program is the best option and will definitely help you get a job quickly. You should apply right away because it is affordable and flexible.
  • No audience, program, source, or success criteria.
  • Makes promises about employment.
  • No link to official information.
  • No human judgment or review note.
  • Too vague to verify.

Sample B: CLEAR AI-Assisted Work

Task brief:

Draft a short, plain-language email for a prospective student asking where to learn more about a public SMCC program. Use only the public webpage text pasted below. Do not promise admission, financial aid, licensure, employment, or transfer. Include a reminder to contact the official office for current details. Flag anything missing instead of inventing it.

Human review note:

  • Checked that the draft avoids promises.
  • Needs official link and contact before use.
  • Uses only public information.
  • Final send remains the staff member's responsibility.

Optional Discussion Questions

  • What did the clearer task brief prevent?
  • What still needs a human check?
  • What would change if the message included student-specific information?
  • How could this same standard apply to an assignment, SOP, code review, or patient education handout?
