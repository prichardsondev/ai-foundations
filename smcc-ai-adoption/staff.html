Staff Handout

Practical AI guidance for SMCC operations

Staff Agency Framing

Staff expertise matters. AI can help draft, summarize, organize, and simplify work, but it should not quietly make decisions that belong to accountable SMCC people. Policy-related boundaries are pending SMCC governance review.

Human judgment remains accountable.

Safe Uses of AI for Staff

Use only public, internal, synthetic, or data approved by the relevant SMCC data owner, IT/security, accessibility, procurement, or governance process, and verify outputs before sharing.

  • Draft plain-language explanations from public information or approved materials.
  • Create first drafts of SOPs or checklists.
  • Summarize non-sensitive meeting notes.
  • Improve clarity, tone, or readability.
  • Generate questions for planning or process review.
  • Draft training outlines from approved materials.

Unsafe or Prohibited Uses Pending Approval

Do not enter confidential/regulated data into unapproved tools.

  • Student records, grades, advising notes, retention alerts, conduct issues, or financial aid details.
  • Employee records, performance, hiring, payroll, medical leave, investigations, or accommodation data.
  • Disability/accommodation documentation or student health/disability narratives.
  • Clinical-like cases tied to real people.
  • Public safety incident details, locations, victims, witnesses, or active incident information.
  • Passwords, security credentials, Social Security numbers, bank data, or medical identifiers.
  • Proprietary partner information or confidential vendor/procurement information.

Role-Specific Guardrails

  • Admissions: do not use AI to determine admission status or make promises about admission.
  • Advising: do not enter student academic records into unapproved tools; generated plans are not authoritative.
  • Financial aid: do not enter financial aid records or personal financial data into unapproved tools; do not promise aid outcomes.
  • Accessibility: do not enter disability/accommodation data into external AI tools; use approved language without identifiers.
  • HR: do not use unapproved AI for employment decisions, investigations, performance reviews, or employee records.
  • Facilities: avoid exposing safety, location, access, or incident details in unapproved tools.
  • IT: do not enter credentials, sensitive logs, vulnerabilities, or security incident details into unapproved tools.
  • Student support: keep outreach human-reviewed; do not automate consequential student decisions.

Reflection Questions

  • What repetitive work might AI help draft or organize safely?
  • What data do I handle that should never go into external AI tools?
  • What human decision must remain accountable in my workflow?
  • How will I verify the output before another person sees it?
  • What policy, tool approval, or privacy question do I need answered?
